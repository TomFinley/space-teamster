# # Estella IV, Dahai (大海)

Covered by a water ocean a hundred kilometers deep till what one might charitably call stone, Dahai truly is a Great Sea. Fourth in position, and last settled, of the worlds of the hearth. A world of water, storms, and where life, tenuous though it may be, has found a fragile foothold.

As near as we can tell, all native life on this world are anaerobic extremophiles, living within the Ice VI and Ice VII slush found about 100 kilometers beneath the liquid surface of this planet. 1. Bīngjié (冰界) – The Ice Boundary
The Gateway to the Stars
Perched on the massive, floating frozen continents of the poles, Bīngjié is the only "stable" ground on the planet. It serves as the primary Starport, where the atmosphere is thinnest and the hydrogen layer is most accessible for refueling.

Role: Planetary logistics, interstellar trade, and deep-freeze data archiving.

Environment: A clinical, high-altitude desert of blinding white frost and silent server farms.

The Challenge: A precision-heavy landing zone where thin air meets frictionless ice, requiring pilots to master instrument-only landings during blinding white-out conditions.

2. Jiǔyuān (九淵, The Nine Abysses)
The Industrial Anchor
A massive, semi-submerged needle built directly over a primary hydrothermal plume, Jiǔyuān reaches a mile into the air and sixty miles into the dark. It is the heart of the planet's resource economy, siphoning phosphorus and rare minerals from the core's edge.

Role: Mineral extraction, heavy refining, and chemical processing.

Environment: A high-pressure industrial hive of matte-black ceramic and glowing orange pressure gauges, humming with the vibration of deep-sea pumps.

The Challenge: A tight-quarters docking maneuver into a vertical spindle, complicated by magnetic interference and violent wind-vortices shed by the massive structure.

3. Fúyuàn (浮苑) – The Floating Garden
**The Living Lung**
A lush, sprawling network of bio-engineered "lily-pads" that drift along equatorial currents. Fúyuàn is a masterpiece of aquaculture, utilizing the planet's abiotic oxygen and siphoned minerals to grow Earth-origin flora and fauna.

Role: Oxygen production, gourmet aquaculture (Tuna/Salmon), and high-tier culture.

Environment: A humid, fragrant paradise of emerald kelp curtains and hanging gardens, lit by the soft, diffused violet light of the sky-fire.

The Challenge: A "soft-touch" approach requiring extreme thermal management to avoid scorching the delicate, living infrastructure with thruster exhaust.

4. Zhúlóng (逐龍, The Dragon-Chaser)
**The Storm Reaper**
A rugged, armored city in constant motion, Zhúlóng does not avoid the planet's super-cyclones—it hunts them. By positioning itself in the wake of "Dragons," it harvests the massive nutrient upwellings and biological "slurry" churned up from the depths.

Role: Mass protein production and harvesting of native extremophiles.

Environment: A gritty, metallic frontier of "Storm-Runners" living amidst 50-meter waves and the constant roar of the gale.

The Challenge: The "Cowboy Landing"—a high-speed intercept with a moving, pitching deck in the middle of an atmospheric electrical storm.

Dahai. I've learnt a small bit of the language -- great sea. Guess it fits. A world at even its shallowest consists of impossibly deep oceans that have, for all practical purposes, no bottom. Seismographic studies have revealed the structure of this planet and they all confirm that the solid core of this planet, if such a thing exists -- and indeed it must -- must consist of only 10% of the planet's radius, and so perhaps, we'll say, 0.1% of its volume, and so maybe, if we are feeling generous, 1% of its mass. Of course such a calculation of how water might behave under such extraordinary pressures would require the speculation of entirely new ices. Which I have done.

Dahai is my homeworld. I come from Shanghai. Mmmm, funny that. You know I hear my Chinese friends tell me about how unoriginal Americans were when it came to naming cities. "Spring-field" and "Ithaca" and "Ontario" and suchlike. And even when we name places like "Seattle" or "Vancouver" they -- well it's a useless argument. My point is, what do they do when they find a water planet? They name the first city and capital ... Shanghai. Literally just "Upon the Sea." So it is, they're not wrong, but Christ. You want to bust my chops about how Americans reused names, that's fine, but don't bust my chops when the best you can do is just reuse the name Shanghai.

The three belt cities are built the same -- simple lily-pad style. A broad, very broad base at the bottom. Then a more narrow litoral zone just some meters below. Then the city itself is built, quite naturally, above the sea level itself. As always. How else would one build a city?
